I teach in the Department of Geography and the Environment at the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill.

## GEOG 215: Introduction to Spatial Data Science

GEOG 215 introduces spatial data science: using code to collect, manage, analyze, and map data about places, and asking why location matters. Students work in R and RStudio from the first week, learning to think geographically and computationally at the same time.

The course moves through six units:

- **R and spatial data science:** R, RStudio, and R Markdown; vectors, data frames, if statements, loops, and writing your own functions.
- **The tidyverse:** filtering, sorting, and summarizing data with dplyr and the pipe, and making graphs with ggplot2.
- **Maps and geographic data:** thematic maps with tmap, vector and raster data, coordinate systems and map projections, and downloading data from the US Census Bureau and ArcGIS servers.
- **Spatial analysis:** joining tables by attribute and by location, exploratory data analysis, and spatial statistics such as the mean center.
- **Statistics and science:** p-values, ANOVA, linear regression, reproducible projects, and spatial autocorrelation with Moran's I and Getis-Ord Gi\*.
- **Next steps:** interactive maps and dashboards with leaflet and Shiny, and where spatial data science leads, from remote sensing to GeoAI.

### Textbook

The course has its own free, interactive textbook, written from the lectures. Every chapter is a runnable Quarto document, so the tables and maps come from the R code printed beside them, and many code boxes can be edited and run right in your browser.

## GEOG 370: Geographic Information Science

GEOG 370 introduces geographic data and the core concepts of Geographic Information Science. Students learn how spatial data is built, managed, and represented, where it comes from and what its limits are, and how to work with it in QGIS and ArcGIS Pro. There are no prerequisites, so it suits students who are new to GIS as well as those who already have some experience. The course counts toward the IDEAs in Action FC-QUANT or FC-NATSCI requirement.

The course moves through three units, and each one ends with a project built on real-world data:

- **Data models and projections:** getting started with GIS, working with geographic information, and coordinate systems and map projections. The project is editing and querying spatial data.
- **Spatial data quality:** working with US Census data and georeferencing. The project is mapping Census data.
- **Spatial analysis:** density analysis, measurement, and weather data. The project is a GIS project of your own.

### Textbook

The course has its own free, interactive open textbook, written from the lectures. Chapters explain the concepts behind geographic information and show how to put them into practice in QGIS and ArcGIS Pro. New chapters are being added as they are written.
