New degree program

Put your data on the map: a new B.S. in Geospatial Data Science

The new B.S. launched this fall for students who like technology, coding, and spatial analytics and want to point them at real problems in sustainability, social justice, and global health. It takes data out of spreadsheets and puts it on the map.

Students track climate change, analyze satellite imagery, map disease outbreaks, and design resilient cities. They get hands-on with Python, R, GIS software, Google Earth Engine, and machine learning, and build interactive maps, dashboards, and spatial models.

Student research, internships, and course spotlight
RESEARCH | Addison Lioy, Geography major, GIS and Public Policy minors (Dec 2027)
How Charlotte community organizations respond to federal immigration enforcement

Mentored by Dr. Nina Martin and funded by a Summer Undergraduate Research Fellowship (SURF), Addison did a summer project examining community identity and protest of federal immigration enforcement.

Trying to meet community members, cold emails drew few replies, so she showed up in person at pop-up protests and community meetings and recruited far more participants. Interviewees drew their communities on county maps; she georeferenced them in QGIS, merged the polygons, and tied spatial layers to coded interview themes. Congrats to Addison on her great project!

Geographic Centers of Self-Defined Community, Addison Lioy.

Addison’s advice: talk to someone about a cause you care about. UNC Geography faculty are open and welcoming to excited students.

INTERNSHIP | Charlotte Zimmerman, Geospatial Data Science major (May 2027)
From City of Hickory utilities to the USDA Forest Service

LinkedIn and Handshake went nowhere, so Charlotte went straight to municipal websites. She found an internship with the city of Hickory over the summer. She built a portfolio first by mapping for the non-profit Let Us Lead, which led to work on local city council campaigns.

In Hickory she edited attributes in ArcGIS Pro, digitized hundreds of historical paper maps, made ward and trail maps, and spent one day a week at construction sites using ArcGIS Field Maps and ground-penetrating radar to update water and sewer data. Next: digitizing and mapping historic cemeteries in Kentucky for the Forest Service from 1950s USGS topo maps.

Charlotte’s advice: if job boards fail, do GIS projects for people you know, so you have something besides coursework to show.

COURSE | GEOG 515 Applied Spatial Data Science, with Alex Henson (PhD, year 2)
Forty years of climate trends in one script

GEOG 515: Applied Spatial Data Science is taught by Dr. Paul Delamater, and the course showed Alex how R goes beyond point-and-click desktop GIS with open-source, reproducible workflows. Hundreds of gigabytes of data and 40 years of climate trends run from a single automated script. “The beauty of R”, Alex says, “is that you aren’t bound by what tools the software offers but rather have the ability to create any analysis you could imagine.”

His project combined land cover, tree canopy, impervious surface, and personal weather station data to model 30-meter land temperature and relative humidity for Miami-Dade County. In GEOG 591 with Dr. Amanda DelVecchia, he extended it to mold index estimates by census block group in ArcGIS.

Left: 30 Meter Mold Index Over Miami-Dade County, June 2023. Right: Modeling Land Temperature from PWS Observations. By Alex Henson.

Alex’s advice: If you’re looking for a cool class, talk to your professors. They are just people!
