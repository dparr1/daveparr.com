About the author

I’m Dr. David Parr, a Teaching Assistant Professor in the Department of Geography and the Environment at the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill. I teach classes in spatial data science, GIS, programming, web mapping, spatial databases, cartography, spatial modeling and analysis, and data acquisition and integration.

I see my disciplines, Geographic Information Science (GIS) and spatial data science, as the intersections of Geography and Computer Science, the fields in which I have earned my degrees. I want students to be able to think geographically: to critically consider the dynamic spatial and temporal issues of a problem, and to consider how scale and distance affect our human and physical environments. I also want them to think computationally: to break problems into smaller components in a logical sequence, and to consider how a representation in a computer is related to the object in the real world. These skills, understanding spatial relations and logical problem solving, are critical in our discipline and in the wider world.

My teaching and research interests include volunteered geographic information, space-time spatial analysis and modeling, GIS and geography education, and GIS and society, including digital places and web GIS.

About this book

This book is a working draft, written from the lectures for GEOG 215. It is a Quarto book: every chapter is a runnable document, so the tables, maps, and figures come from the R code printed beside them, and many code boxes can be edited and run right in your browser.
